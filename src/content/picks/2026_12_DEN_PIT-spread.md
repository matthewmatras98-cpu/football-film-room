The Steelers have Mason Rudolph listed as the starter this week. Aaron Rodgers has taken nearly every dropback this season, and Rudolph has just four all year. That's a backup on a short week, the day after Thanksgiving, against a Denver team that's won six straight. I don't need much more than that, and the last two Pittsburgh games don't help their case.

They've scored 18 and 16 in back-to-back losses to Cincinnati and Philadelphia, dropping to 6–4. Over those two games, the offense ranks 23rd in EPA and 27th in success rate. Cincinnati wasn't a total collapse, since they were about average per play and still lost 38–18. But Philadelphia was ugly, minus 0.13 EPA per play, and they're 30th in red-zone touchdown rate this season. I don't trust this offense to stay on schedule with a backup on a five-day turnaround.

Meanwhile, the Broncos offense is ascending. They're 8–2 and have scored 30, 28, 26, and 38 over their last four. Since week 7 they're 5th in offensive EPA after ranking 11th for the season, and their success rate has jumped from 26th to 9th. Bo Nix has climbed from 16th in EPA per dropback to 9th over that stretch, and Denver's offense has finished with positive EPA in each of those four games. Pittsburgh's defense has been solid this year, 8th in EPA allowed, but it hasn't been enough the last two weeks. I want the offense that's trending up, even as a road favorite.

The game is outdoors at Acrisure, and the weather might be rough. I wouldn't build this pick on Denver running the Steelers over, though. The Broncos' rushing EPA is up to 8th over the last four, but they almost never break big runs, ranking 27th in explosive-run rate in that stretch. Pittsburgh's run defense is 2nd in rush EPA allowed over the same span. If this turns into a grind, that matchup isn't in Denver's favor. The bigger issue is Pittsburgh having to throw with a backup while Denver's offense keeps rolling.

The pick: Denver −2.5 on the road Friday.
