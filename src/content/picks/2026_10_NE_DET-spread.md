Detroit has the better offense, and I don't think it's close. The Lions are seventh in the league in EPA per play this season, and they hit explosive plays on almost 12% of snaps over their last four games. That's a lot of chunk plays. Jared Goff has cooled a little from his hot start, but he's still producing about 0.15 EPA per dropback over the last month, and this offense doesn't need him to be perfect.

The bigger story is what Detroit is facing. New England's defense has fallen apart lately. Over the Patriots' last four games, opponents have averaged 0.109 EPA per play against them, which ranks 28th in the league, and 11% of the plays they allow go for explosives. Now they're also without corner Carlton Davis III. A Lions offense that thrives on big plays gets a secondary that's already been giving them up.

Meanwhile the Lions defense keeps getting better. Early in the year it was a problem: opponents put up 0.44 EPA per play in Week 2 and 0.29 in Week 4. Over the last four games, Detroit has allowed −0.055 EPA per play, and third-down conversions against it have dropped from 44% on the season to 36%. To be fair, the last two came against Minnesota and Miami, but the trend holds back to Week 5.

That's why I struggle to see Drake Maye keeping pace. The Patriots' offense sits around the middle of the league, 15th in EPA per play over the last month, and they've been bad at finishing drives. They've scored touchdowns on just 38% of red-zone trips this season, one of the weakest rates in the league. Kicking field goals isn't going to keep up with a Lions offense that can score from anywhere.

Detroit's pass rush has to force Maye into mistakes, and the timing is good for that. Maye holds the ball longer than almost anyone at about 3.3 seconds to throw over his last four starts, which gives a rush time to get there. New England is also missing starting center Ben Brown. Detroit's defense brings down the quarterback on about 7.5% of dropbacks, and the Lions don't need many sacks to stall a drive. They need to make Maye uncomfortable.

The Patriots' passing game runs through Demario Douglas and Hunter Henry, and Maye has been accurate when he gets time. If Detroit takes away that time, I don't think New England's receivers win enough one-on-ones to make up for it.

**The pick:** Lions −3. Detroit has the better offense and a defense that's trending the right way, and it's facing a New England defense that has been leaking big plays. If the Lions win by a field goal or more, I like it.
