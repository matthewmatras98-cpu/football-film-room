I think the Vikings defense is going to swallow Michael Penix whole. This pass rush isn't just good, it's the best in football. Minnesota is sacking quarterbacks on 11.3% of dropbacks this season, first in the league, and that's up to 13.1% over the last four. If you count a sack or a QB hit as pressure, they're first there too, getting home on about a quarter of opponent dropbacks.

And Penix is exactly the kind of quarterback you want that group hunting. With a clean pocket he's been roughly average, about 0.04 EPA per dropback with 62% completions. Once he gets hit or sacked, it falls off a cliff: minus 1.36 EPA per dropback, and he's completed 1 of 7 throws. The catch is it hasn't happened often. He's been pressured on about 7% of his dropbacks, the lowest rate in the league, and he gets the ball out in 2.63 seconds, third-fastest among starters. So this only works if Minnesota gets there. I think they can. It's the best pressure defense in the league against a quarterback who looks lost the moment the pocket isn't perfect.

Even with a clean pocket lately, Atlanta has been sputtering. Over their last four, Penix is at minus 0.23 EPA per dropback and minus 9.1 CPOE, 28th and 26th among starters. The Falcons lost by 16, 17, and 22 before the bye, including a 16–0 loss in Tampa. They've converted just 28% of third downs in that stretch, 31st in the league, and they're scoring touchdowns on only 31% of red-zone trips. This isn't just a pressure story. The whole operation is stuck.

I don't expect the Vikings offense to have to score much, so I'm not too worried about that side. Yes, they just got wrecked 35–10 in San Francisco, and this offense ranks 30th in EPA. But Minnesota's defense is still first in EPA allowed and first in red-zone touchdown rate allowed, letting opponents score on only 27% of trips inside the 20. They're 7–3 because that defense has carried them. Atlanta is 3–7, 27th in offensive EPA, and on a three-game slide. I see Minnesota getting after Penix at home, the Falcons stalling out on drives, and the Vikings never having to trade punches.

The pick: Minnesota −7 at home Sunday.
